## What I Built

Tamagoshi Outdoor is a small companion app for adults who spend most of the day at a desk. You type a few
interests, pick 5, 10 or 15 minutes, and a local Gemma model proposes three tiny outdoor missions: notice the
shadows in a plaza, listen for three different birds, look at the patterns in the pavement. Then you close the
laptop and go.

When you come back you can write one line about what you noticed. The mission moves to a discovery journal and
you get a few points. That's the whole loop. No streaks, no notifications, no account, no GPS, and nothing
punishes you for skipping a day.

These are real missions Gemma 3 4B generated on my laptop during testing:

> **Shadow Play.** Seek a shaded area in the park. Observe the shadows cast by nearby trees and structures.
> Consider how the shadows shift and change with the passing time.

> **Pattern in the Pavement.** Walk along the street, focusing on the patterns in the pavement. Identify
> repeating shapes, lines, or textures. Notice how they change with the light.

> **Silencio en el Parque.** Busca un lugar tranquilo en el parque. Cierra los ojos y concéntrate en los
> sonidos naturales: el viento, los pájaros, el agua. Respira profundamente y aprecia el momento de calma y la
> naturaleza.

The UI works in Spanish and English, because I wanted to use it myself in Spanish.

## How I Built It

- **Model:** `gemma3:4b` served locally by [Ollama](https://ollama.com). It runs on my laptop's CPU only.
- **Backend:** Python + FastAPI + SQLite. One endpoint asks Gemma for missions, validates them, and stores them.
- **Frontend:** React + Vite, a single page, mobile-first.

The interesting part was making a 4B model's output trustworthy enough to show without a human in the loop:

1. **Strict validation.** Gemma must return exactly three missions as JSON. A Pydantic schema checks types and
   lengths, rejects duplicate titles, markup, control characters and leaked JSON, and runs a small English/Spanish
   denylist (private property, roofs, traffic, spending money, GPS…).
2. **One retry, then an honest fallback.** If the output is invalid, the backend asks once more. If it is still
   invalid, or Ollama is unreachable, the user gets hand-written demo missions with a visible **DEMO** badge and a
   message explaining why. The API reports `fallback_reason: "ai_unavailable" | "ai_invalid_output"`.
3. **Prompt-injection hygiene.** Interests are free text, so they are stripped of quotes, brackets and newlines and
   wrapped in `<interests>` tags the model is told to treat as themes only. Even if the model obeys an injected
   instruction, the output still has to pass validation.

Things that went wrong, and what I changed:

- My first prompt showed a one-item JSON example. In Spanish Gemma returned three missions anyway; in English it
  consistently returned **one**. Showing a three-item example fixed it.
- I then tried Ollama's structured output (`format` with a JSON schema). It forced three items, but Gemma started
  padding strings with junk such as `“}]} ,{"` and `0x0000…`, and that junk was *inside* valid strings, so it passed
  my first validator. Plain `format: "json"` returned HTTP 500 in 3 of 6 calls. Dropping `format` entirely, extracting
  the JSON object myself and validating it strictly gave 5 of 6 valid answers in a row, and the retry covers the rest.
- Gemma kept naming real places ("Plaza Mayor de Salamanca", "Jardín Botánico de Madrid") that had nothing to do with
  where I am. One line in the prompt ("use generic nearby places, never name specific cities, parks or venues") fixed it.
- On CPU a generation takes about 40–85 s. The first version used a 12 s timeout, so it silently fell back to demo
  missions every time. Now the timeout is 120 s, the UI says "this can take a minute or two", and nginx's proxy
  timeout is raised to match.
- Completing a mission was a `SELECT` followed by an `UPDATE`. Eight concurrent requests produced a 500 from the
  database's unique constraint. A single conditional `UPDATE … WHERE completed_at IS NULL` awards points exactly once.

The backend has 64 pytest tests, including invalid-model-output cases, concurrent completions, a real server restart
to check persistence, CORS and injection cases. I also checked contrast ratios (WCAG AA), focus styles and keyboard
order in the UI.

## Why Open Innovation Matters Here

- **It runs with Wi-Fi off.** The model, the app and the data are all on the laptop. TODO: confirm you recorded the
  demo offline and say so here.
- **Your habits stay yours.** What you're curious about and what you wrote in your journal never leave the machine.
  There's no server I control, so there is nothing for me to collect.
- **It costs nothing per suggestion.** No API key, no quota, no bill.
- **I could look inside and change it.** Because the model is open and local, I could test its failure modes
  directly, compare output modes, tune the prompt and temperature, and swap models by changing one environment
  variable (`OLLAMA_MODEL`).
- **Where closed would have been "better":** a hosted frontier model would answer in a couple of seconds instead of
  ~50 s on my CPU and would rarely break the JSON format. For a nudge you read once and then close, I'll take slower
  and private.
